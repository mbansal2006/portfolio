# Why Things Must Happen

We’ll often say “everything happens for a reason.” It’s often emotional — to comfort someone, to soften something painful, to make sense of chaos. Sometimes we need that.

A lot of the time, we say it in retrospect. Like, this super unlikely thing (for better or worse) happened, and one thing led to another, and… here I am. We emphasize how weird or improbable it was. But what if it wasn’t weird? What if it was the only way it could’ve happened?

What if “everything happens for a reason” isn’t just some platitude — but a structural truth?
Not spiritual. Not moral. Not emotional. 

Just… geometric.

What if the universe isn’t something that unfolds — but something that just is? What if time doesn’t flow — it’s just another axis, like up/down, forward/backward, or left-right? And what we call events are just points in a four-dimensional shape — already set, already there, already consistent?

In that case, things wouldn’t happen because they’re “meant to” in some mystical sense. They’d happen because they’re the only thing that can happen in that part of the shape.

I'm thinking of this all as a kind of function. A huge, four-dimensional one — call it U(x, y, z, t).
Every tuple is an event. What's where and when. It's not random. It's not flexible. It's just whatever the function returns at that input.

If you plug in a specific (x, y, z, t) — a specific moment — you get exactly one result. No ambiguity. No rerolling the dice. Just: this is what happens here.

It’s like asking, “What’s the value of sin(π/2)?” There’s no room for debate. It’s 1. That’s the geometry. So when anything happens, incredible or tragic, it’s not because someone decided it should. It’s because that’s just the point in the shape.

It might feel wild or unpredictable from the inside. It might feel needless. But from the outside, it’s consistent. It's a geometric necessity. It had to happen - not because it’s good or bad, but because it’s how the structure holds together.

That’s what this is: a guess and a dream. A way of seeing everything - as messy and painful and strange as it can be — as part of a single, coherent structure.

Maybe it’s close to the truth. I don’t think we’d have any real way of knowing. I certainly don’t.
But my point is: I hope it is.

Because if it is — if the shape really holds — then there’s some peace in that. I hope every event, no matter how meaningless or chaotic it feels in the moment, is actually doing something essential. I hope it’s holding the universe together.

At best, it's realistic. At worst, it's a pretty neat story to live in.

## Events as Geometric Necessities

I think we often imagine the universe as something that moves — something that’s always going forward. Like a system evolving frame by frame. That’s basically the backdrop for my first essay on this blog, "The Endurance of Intelligence" thinking about how intelligence will evolve over time.

But now I’m trying to take a step back.

What if the universe isn’t changing at all? What if it’s a shape — some four-dimensional structure made what we call space and time — and every moment is just a point in that shape? And what we experience as time is just the path we trace across it?

You can imagine it like a 4-row matrix with infinite columns: three for space (x, y, z), and one for time (t). The whole thing already exists. We’re just scanning it.

It’s like walking across a mosaic that stretches infinitely in every direction. The pattern doesn’t build itself — it’s already there. You just happen to be standing on a particular tile, looking around.

Or like slicing through a solid object with a plane — each slice is a moment. Each intersection is what you experience. Our lives would just be curves in that shape, tracing every point we experience. But the object itself never changes. It just holds.

Nothing could’ve been worse. Nothing could’ve been better. Everything that happened had to be the way it was because it was the only way the structure could stay intact.

Each moment fits. Not righteously. But structurally. It’s not just that everything happens for a reason. It’s that everything is a reason — each point held in place by all the others.

That doesn’t mean you have no agency. But maybe it means that your agency — your choices, your memories, your mistakes — are part of the structure too. And you couldn’t have made them any other way.

That’s what gives things weight. That’s what gives them meaning. Not because someone planned it, but because every moment, every "action" holds the universe together.

## Memory and Imagination (Intelligence at its Core?)

I’ve basically said that time is this dimension of the shape we can’t see — so we experience the universe as three-dimensional. But that’s not really true. We feel time.

We remember things. We reflect. We regret. We imagine. We dream. We loop over the past, reach toward the future, revise things that never happened.

Every memory is a kind of echo — a way of briefly lighting up another part of the structure, revisiting a coordinate we no longer occupy. Every dream, every innovation, every flicker of foresight, might be a glimpse of a region in the shape we’d call “the future” — but in reality, it’s already there, just like we are here.

In that sense, memory and imagination are the only things that let us see more of the shape. More than what’s directly in front of us. They give us range.

Maybe that’s what intelligence is. Not just awareness, but a kind of range —
an ability to move across the shape, not physically, but cognitively. To reach other points in time. To light up coordinates beyond the present moment. To see more of the structure than we otherwise could.

I feel like intelligence is a property of the shape itself. Like curvature. Or distance from the center. Not something added, but something baked in to each point. Something that we experience as seeing more.

And maybe the more we remember, the more we imagine, the more of the shape we carry inside us. The more we become able to reflect not just on what has happened, but on what had to. Maybe at some point, we can feel the see the whole shape.

## Interstellar and Tesseracts

This reminded me of Interstellar, which a lot of people think of when talking about time as a dimension. That movie got really close to this idea.

In the final act, the main character (Cooper) falls into what they call “the tesseract” — a space where he can see across time, like all the different versions of his daughter’s bedroom stacked and spread out. It’s emotional. It’s cool. But also… it’s not really what a tesseract is.

Geometrically, a tesseract is just a four-dimensional cube. It’s the next step up from a 3D cube — same logic, one more dimension. You can’t see it directly (just like how you can't see a 3D cube on paper), but it’s a well-defined shape.

The movie used “tesseract” to mean something else: a kind of fifth-dimensional construct built by mysterious higher beings so a human could perceive time spatially. It worked for the plot, but it still relied on a narrative — someone built it, someone designed it to help someone else.

This was elegant storytelling. But it wasn’t geometry.

The film still needed a creator — higher-dimensional beings who “built” the tesseract to save humanity. It needed a hero. A message. A reason.

But in my dream, there isn't anyone behind the scenes. No fifth-dimensional saviors. No “they.” The infinitely large tesseract is enough.

## God

And if the shape is all there is — if everything exists already, perfectly fixed — then maybe God isn’t a creator or observer or judge. Maybe God is just… the shape itself. Or some property of it.

Not a being with thoughts or plans, but a property of the universe. Like symmetry. Or mass. Or π in a circle. God is what holds everything together. The thing that makes the structure complete.

That’s why people say “God is everywhere.” Maybe that’s not just a metaphor. Maybe we’re all part of the same shape. Maybe that’s what divinity is: totality.

And maybe that’s why God doesn’t interfere. Not because God isn’t real — but because interference would be a contradiction. Because if God is the structure, then God can’t break the shape to fix a part of it. God doesn’t act because God is what stays still. Because the shape is still.

God is the part of the universe that never moves — because it already contains everything. Maybe that’s the answer to the "Problem of Evil". Not a test. Not a mystery. Just a system that only makes sense as a whole.

## Heaven

If time is just a dimension, then so is the future. And if the universe is fixed, then I wonder if — somewhere in the structure — heaven already exists. Not as a magical realm above us, but as a region.
A part of the shape where intelligence has evolved far enough to remember everything. Not just facts, but meaning. Understanding. Peace.

Maybe it’s just a region of the shape we get closer and closer to. It might be infinitely far away, but in the logic of the shape, it’s already there. Already real. Already etched into the structure. 

And maybe it looks nothing like we expect. Maybe it’s not a place where we live in some spiritual sense. Maybe it’s a point where all matter — everything that once thought, felt, or existed — decomposes and reforms into something else: a kind of super-consciousness that spans the entire shape, able to remember it all. To reflect on every moment at once. A being made not of bodies, but of memory.

Maybe heaven is just the far end of intelligence. Where awareness becomes total. Where nothing is lost.

Not because someone built it, but because the geometry allowed for it. Because given enough time, the structure curves that way.

At least, that's what I like to imagine it as.

## Final Thoughts

We all live with some version of this question "Why?". "Why now?". "Why did that happen?".

We all go through things that feel chaotic, unfair, or meaningless — moments that shake the idea that the world makes sense at all. Times we feel we're mad at God or the universe or whatever.

I'm not religous, but I grew up I doing Aaarti (Hindu Ritual) every night. I went to temple multiple time a week. That led to nine year-old me feeling cheated. I forget my exact words, but I believe I said something along the line of "either God is dead, or he doesn't care and I don't care about him".

I heard my little sister say something similar when she was around that age, when my mom died. At that point, I think my perspective shifted. I disagreed with my sister and nine year-old me. Things didn't seem chaotic in the disordered sense, I felt like though they appeared chaotic they followed some order. It was an order I'd probably not understand but one that I was confident existed.

I only mention this to explain two related dreams. 

First, I hope that though things appear chaotic, there is an order. Some shape. Some matrix. Some function. I hope that even the worst moments weren’t for nothing. That maybe they didn’t make sense to us, but they made sense to the shape. That they were necessary. That they held the universe together.

Second, I hope that this system appears less chaotic in some region of the shape. I hope that as we scan through this shape, at some point, we understand the order. We see it. Things make sense to us just as they make sense to the shape.

I don't think any of this should mean you should accept everything without feeling. Or stop asking why. But it might help to remember: the world isn’t glitching. It’s just geometry. Strange, beautiful, brutal geometry.

And maybe the only way to understand that geometry is to remember and imagine. To reflect. To innovate. To look across time.
